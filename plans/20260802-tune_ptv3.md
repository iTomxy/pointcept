## Caveat: the tuned lr is not stable

Steps 1 and 1b pin lr at 0.005719 for every run, so those rows are clean. From step 2 onward each run re-tunes its own lr with `tools/lr_range_test.py`, and the `lr` column shows the result spans **0.0045 to 0.0183, a 4x range**, across runs that were supposed to differ only in the factor under test.

That spread is mostly not a real per-arm optimum. Comparing the range-test curves of `augc-all5` (lr 0.0051) and `opt-ref` (lr 0.0115) -- configs that resolve byte-identical -- the two loss curves agree to 3 decimals up to lr 0.012 and then diverge: one hit a transient loss spike just past 0.012 and never recovered its minimum, the other did not. Both curves agree on where training actually blows up (~0.08), but the `lr_min_loss`/`lr_knee` heuristic latches onto the local blip instead, so a single noisy pass moves the recommendation by 2.25x.

Practical effect: comparisons between rows with similar lr are sound, and rows whose lr differs by >2x carry an unquantified confound -- flagged in the notes where it matters. `opt-ref` measures the size of that confound directly, since it is the `all5` recipe at 2.25x the lr and nothing else changed.

Read the `-lrpin` rows: three of the four arms drew a higher lr than `augc-all5`, the 3mm-fp32 cell they are all measured against. At matched lr the grid is 3mm 0.9336 (fp32/reduced) > 4mm/full/bf16 0.9314 > 3mm/full/bf16 0.9290 > 4mm/reduced/fp32 0.9280 > 3mm/reduced/bf16 0.9238, and each factor is now clean in isolation: **bf16 costs 0.98 pt**, **full windows gain 0.52 pt**, **4mm costs 0.56 pt**. So **keep 3mm and keep fp32** (restored, on the one-variable fp32/reduced comparison), but **"`enc_patch_size` is irrelevant" was wrong** -- the original -0.02 tie was two opposing errors cancelling. It changes nothing in practice: full windows in fp32 are unreachable (no flash means the attention matrix is materialised, memory linear in patch size, OOM at 8x), so fp32 forces reduced windows, and the +0.52 never covers the -0.98. The factors are still not additive -- stacking all three predicts -1.02 and measures -0.23 -- but the earlier claim that each *flips sign* depending on the other was itself an lr artefact. 2.5mm stays excluded on measurement regardless: 96% of a 22GB card AND stage4_max 1042 > 1024, which loses the single global attention window at the deepest stage.

**Every knob is a tie: label smoothing, weight decay and drop_path all leave the result unchanged.** Weight decay is the flattest -- a 100x sweep (5e-4 -> 5e-2) moves the fourth decimal, which is a stronger null than scattered ties would be. `dp0.5` looked like the one winner at +0.36 pt with every metric agreeing, so it got a second seed: the two `dp0.5` runs differ by **0.41 pt from each other**, independently reproducing the 0.40 pt noise floor, and the mean lands +0.16. The first seed was a high draw. Read `-s7` rows as second seeds, not variants.

Taken with steps 1 and 3, this is the pattern of the whole sweep: input features inert, optimisation knobs inert, **augmentation worth +2.96 pt**. On 320 training volumes the limit is data variety, not regularisation.
